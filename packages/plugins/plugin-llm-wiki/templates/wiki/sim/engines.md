# SIM Engines

Engines are the value-producing parts of a company. In user-facing product language they can appear as familiar roles or work areas, but the SIM Wiki should keep their system meaning explicit.

## Product Engine

The Product Engine turns insight into an offer, roadmap, delivery system, and quality loop.

Watch for:

- product work without customer evidence
- delivery promises without capacity checks
- feature output replacing offer clarity

## Customer Engine

The Customer Engine turns audience signal into discovery, trust, demand, and retention.

Watch for:

- customer research treated as optional
- demand assumptions that are not tied to observable behavior
- sales or messaging work disconnected from product learning

## Cash Engine

The Cash Engine turns value creation into pricing, revenue, runway, and reinvestment capacity.

Watch for:

- pricing decisions made without willingness-to-pay evidence
- runway or margin ignored during product expansion
- revenue treated as a lagging scoreboard rather than a design constraint

## Skills Engine

The Skills Engine turns people, agents, routines, and knowledge into increasing capability.

Watch for:

- automation without review or learning
- capability gaps hidden by agent output
- knowledge staying in chats instead of becoming durable memory

## Coach Rule

When a task touches one engine, ask which other engines must stay in the loop. A single-engine plan is often a useful draft, not yet a safe operating plan.
